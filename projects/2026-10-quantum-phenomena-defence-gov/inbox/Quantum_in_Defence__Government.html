Bottom line

The view in seven points

  1. Governments have moved from funding quantum research to buying quantum capability.

    The UK announced up to £2bn on 17 March 2026, including up to £1bn to procure large-scale quantum computers through the ProQure programme (gov.uk). The US took minority equity stakes in nine quantum firms through $2.013bn of CHIPS letters of intent in May 2026 (NIST). NATO's roadmap, released 29 September 2026, turns its 2024 strategy into trials (NATO). Clients will need requirements, evaluation criteria and benchmarking, not just awareness.

  2. Cryptography is the only quantum workstream with a hard external clock, and the clock has sped up.

    Estimated resources to break RSA-2048 fell from about 20 million qubits (2019) to under one million (Gidney, May 2025), with an unproven 2026 preprint claiming under 100,000. US Executive Order 14412 makes PQC key establishment on high-value federal systems due by end-2030 and signatures by end-2031 (Federal Register). The UK's NCSC milestones of 2028, 2031 and 2035 are unchanged and advisory (NCSC). Programmes with US, Australian or EU exposure should plan to the earliest applicable date.

  3. Sensing is closest to the field, but only for clocks and magnetic navigation, and the UK route from trial to programme of record is not visible.

    The Royal Navy ran five quantum PNT trials between June 2025 and June 2026 (Royal Navy). In the US, a DIU and Honeywell flight ran 4h23 without GPS (DefenseScoop). No discrete quantum line was found in the June 2026 Defence Investment Plan To confirm.

  4. Defence computing is a mid-term materials and chemistry story. Near-term optimisation value is quantum-inspired.

    A July 2026 Siemens study found quantum hardware handled only toy scheduling problems while a quantum-inspired annealer competed at industrial scale (arXiv). The DoD Inspector General found the Pentagon's list of quantum-addressable military problems had not been refreshed since 2022 (TQI). DARPA now considers a utility-scale machine by 2033 likely.

  5. Sovereignty and ownership are now design constraints on every vendor choice.

    IonQ bought Oxford Ionics ($1.075bn) and US foundry SkyWater ($1.8bn); the US holds equity in its suppliers; UK, US and EU export controls on quantum items tightened between 2024 and 2025; a 13-nation group convened in London in 2026 prioritised research and investment security (DSIT).

  6. UK defence quantum money competes inside a generic novel-technology envelope.

    The Defence Industrial Strategy and Defence Investment Plan commit at least 10% of the equipment budget to novel technology including AI, autonomy and quantum (Commons Library). Quantum proposals must win benefit cases against AI and autonomy on mission outcomes; PNT resilience and cryptographic risk are the strongest cases.

  7. QKD is not the UK government's answer. Treat networking as a watch brief.

    NCSC states it will not support QKD for government or military applications and names PQC as the primary defence (NCSC, Aug 2025); the Pentagon CIO banned QKD in November 2025. The defence-relevant network threads are entanglement-based time transfer and, longer term, distributed quantum computing.

Maturity map

For computing we use the team's three-tier framing: Quantum Inspired (classical methods drawn from quantum ideas, usable now), Quantum Utility (quantum hardware doing useful work without a clear economic edge) and Quantum Advantage (beating the best classical method on a valuable problem). The tiers were built for computing; sensing, cryptography and networks are rated by deployment readiness instead.

Domain

Computing

Quantum Inspired · nowQuantum Utility · 2026–29Quantum Advantage · ~2029–33
Our view. The credible near-term defence offer is honest problem-mapping, solver benchmarking and quantum-inspired methods, with access and skills built on NQCC and cloud testbeds. First defence-relevant advantage is most plausible in materials and chemistry simulation once machines reach tens to hundreds of logical qubits. Treat any logistics or wargaming advantage claim as unproven until it shows a scaling benefit on real instances.

Hardware has moved from proof of principle to roadmap execution

  • Quantinuum's Helios (Nov 2025) reports 48 error-corrected logical qubits on 98 physical qubits (Quantinuum). Distinguish error-corrected from error-detected counts when assessing vendor claims.
  • IBM targets Starling in 2029 at 200 logical qubits and 100 million gates, and has set itself an end-2026 advantage target with an external tracker (IBM).
  • DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative advanced 11 firms to Stage B in November 2025, including UK-based Quantum Motion (DARPA). PsiQuantum and Microsoft are in the final stage; PsiQuantum's award grew by $125m in July 2026 (PostQuantum). QBI is now the de facto public arbiter of credible roadmaps.

The UK has a national centre and a procurement route, but a thin defence record

  • NQCC has a ten-year, £670m settlement (June 2025) and hosts the UK's only operational 100-qubit system, Infleqtion's Sqale, delivered for March 2026 (NQCC; TQI).
  • ProQure Phase 1 offers contracts of up to £14m (18–24 months, from 1 October 2026), Phase 2 up to £75m, informing a procurement of large machines beyond 2030 (Innovate UK). No MOD access route is described.
  • The public MOD record is the 2022 purchase of ORCA's photonic PT-1 (Information Age), 2023 Dstl hybrid optimisation prototypes built with PA Consulting (HPCwire), a new AWE Quantum Centre for Nuclear Defence and Security (Nov 2025, gov.uk) and a February 2026 QinetiQ and OQC demonstration finding critical nodes in mobile ad-hoc networks (OQC).

Allies are more explicit about defence use

  • France's PROQCIMA defence programme (about €500m) targets 128 logical qubits by 2032 through a staged down-select (TNW); Thales, Bull and CEA launched work on programming quantum computers in classified environments To confirm (TQI).
  • US EO 14413 creates a large quantum computer at a DOE facility and tasks the Department of War with national-security computing (Federal Register). Singapore's defence agencies are piloting IBM-based logistics and mission-planning optimisation (MINDEF).
Domain

Sensing, timing & PNT

Our view. The case for quantum sensing is strongest where GNSS denial is already an operational problem. "Closest to deployment" holds for clocks and magnetic navigation, is weaker for gravimetry and inertial sensors, and does not hold for quantum radar. The UK's gap is not physics or trials; it is the route to a funded requirement, independent test and evaluation, certification, and platform integration. Quantum is one layer in a PNT resilience architecture that is mostly non-quantum (eLoran, national timing).

UK trial cadence is high

  • Aquark's AQlock cold-atom clock ran aboard HMS Puncher (June 2025) and then timed a network of Saab Giraffe 1X radars, letting operators detect simulated spoofing and jamming (trial June 2026, Royal Navy).
  • Infleqtion's Tiqker optical clock ran on the XV Excalibur uncrewed submarine (Oct 2025); Imperial College inertial sensors were trialled in the Arctic (Dec 2025); University of Sussex magnetometers at sea (Oct 2025).
  • Dstl's long-term unattended clock trial leads to a 2027 follow-on, with quantum navigation including atomic clocks targeted for UK aircraft by 2030 (gov.uk, Feb 2026).

UK money is shifting from R&D to adoption and supply chain

  • Innovate UK competitions of up to £14.3m for adoption (IFS) and £32.8m for scaling supply chains (IFS). The adoption call funds removal of commercial, regulatory and integration barriers, which is consulting-shaped scope.
  • The £155m PNT resilience package (Nov 2025) is eLoran, timing and interference monitoring, not quantum (Inside GNSS); a further £180m goes to the National Timing Centre (NPL, Mar 2026). How the two NTC figures relate is To confirm.

The US shows what a fielding pipeline looks like

  • DIU's Transition of Quantum Sensing programme runs field tests across five lines with named vendors (DIU, Mar 2025), followed by an initiative of up to $200m (June 2026). EO 14413 requires three sensor projects fielded by 30 September 2028.
  • DARPA's Robust Quantum Sensors programme (Q-CTRL awarded A$38m) and a pilot manufacturing line for tactical optical clocks (facility mid-2027) signal that manufacturing, not physics, is the bottleneck (TQI).
  • Q-CTRL reports GPS-free gravimetric navigation at sea to about one nautical mile, dependent on pre-loaded gravity maps (vendor) To confirm.

Threat context: a September 2026 Chinese compact magnetometer study did not establish submarine detection (TQI). Monitor, do not plan on it.

Domain

Cryptography & post-quantum migration

Our view. This is the largest and most urgent D&G opportunity, and it is a programme-delivery problem more than a cryptography problem: inventory across IT, OT and platforms, prioritisation by data lifetime and service life, supplier assurance, crypto-agile architecture, and business cases for a migration whose deadlines feel distant. The binding constraints are validated-module supply, supplier readiness and executive attention, not algorithms.

Standards are nearly settled

FIPS 203, 204 and 205 have been final since August 2024 (NIST). FN-DSA (FIPS 206) was still in clearance in April 2026, and draft FIPS 207 for HQC had not appeared by August 2026. NCSC recommends ML-KEM-768 and ML-DSA-65 for general use and hash-based signatures for firmware, with hybrid only as an interim measure (NCSC).

The US defence line is stricter and earlier than the UK's

The Pentagon CIO memo of November 2025 requires a full cryptographic inventory including weapons platforms and OT, named migration leads and approval gates, and bans QKD (PostQuantum). OMB M-26-15 requires migration plans within 120 days and continuously updated, automated inventories with a cryptographic bill of materials (OMB). EO 14412 pushes a FAR rule so contractors follow NIST PQC standards by end-2030, which will flow down to UK suppliers on US programmes.

The UK vehicle is advisory guidance and an assured-consultancy pilot

The NCSC Assured Cyber Security Consultancy PQC pilot runs to 31 March 2027, bought through the Crown Commercial Service (NCSC). The first cohort was reported as eight firms: Arqit, Avella, Cambridge Consultants, Capgemini, CGI, Frazer-Nash, IBM and PQShield (The Stack); a second application window was planned from late spring 2026 (techUK). No public MOD-specific migration programme was found; the Government Cyber Action Plan promises a PQC roadmap (gov.uk). NCSC's July 2026 workshop report names board engagement, supplier alignment and skills as the barriers (NCSC).

Domain

Networks & QKD

Our view. A watch brief, not a proposition. In UK D&G the security conversation is PQC, and advising otherwise would put us against NCSC. Keep track of entanglement-based timing, which overlaps with the PNT agenda, and of distributed quantum computing as the long-run architecture for scaling machines.
  • NCSC will not support QKD for government or military applications; elsewhere it should not be relied on alone and needs quantum-resistant authentication (NCSC, Aug 2025). NSA and the Pentagon take the same line.
  • Commercial and allied activity continues regardless: BT and Toshiba's London quantum-secured metro network (BT), the EU's EuroQCI (EC), and China's 15th Five-Year Plan, which prioritises an integrated space-earth quantum communication network (PostQuantum).
  • UK research assets include the Integrated Quantum Networks hub led by Heriot-Watt (Heriot-Watt) and the UK–Singapore SpeQtre entanglement satellite, reported on orbit in December 2025 To confirm (TQI).
  • Oxford demonstrated a distributed quantum algorithm across two processors linked by a photonic network (Oxford, Feb 2025).
  • NATO's roadmap includes a quantum-resilient communications pilot between Brussels and Mons in Q4 2027; the underlying technology mix is not stated in the coverage seen.
Cross-cutting

Hardware, supply chain & ownership

Our view. Every vendor decision in D&G now carries ownership, export-control and single-source questions. The chokepoints (dilution refrigerators, helium-3, specialist lasers and photonics) bite hardest on computing; sensing depends more on packaging, ruggedisation and photonics, which is exactly what the UK supply-chain competition targets.
  • Export controls: the UK introduced emerging-technology controls including quantum items from April 2024 (Cooley) and added a quantum-computer entry, 4A506, from December 2025 (legislation.gov.uk). IISS argues Western controls are becoming industrial strategy (TQI).
  • Ownership: Oxford Ionics now sits inside IonQ (IonQ), which also owns a US foundry serving defence (Aug 2026). Infleqtion, US-listed, is expanding UK manufacturing in Oxford and cites MOD contracts (Infleqtion).
  • Capital: Lockheed Martin expanded its venture fund to $1bn with $100m for UK and European quantum (Sep 2026). Quantinuum raised $1.68bn in its June 2026 IPO (TQI). McKinsey counts $12.6bn of start-up investment in 2025, only 3% from public sources (McKinsey).
  • Materials: helium-3 supply is being commercialised (TQI); chokepoint analysis in War on the Rocks.

Collaboration map

  • AUKUS Pillar II. Quantum is a named area; the clearest output is an Australian quantum clocks trial in Washington with results shared across partners (Nov 2025). The first signature project (May 2026) is undersea autonomy, not quantum (Squire Patton Boggs).
  • UK–US. The Technology Prosperity Deal (Sep 2025) created a quantum taskforce covering defence (quantum.gov); implementation was reported paused in December 2025 To confirm.
  • Quantum Development Group. 13 nations met in London (Mar–Apr 2026) on research and investment security, supply chains and standards (DSIT).
  • NATO Transatlantic Quantum Community. 22 Allies and 6 partners; Netherlands chair from May 2026; the UK engages through its working groups.
  • UK–Japan. Research-level joint funding (UKRI, Feb 2026) and a computing MoU (Nov 2025).
