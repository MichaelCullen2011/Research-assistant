# Research brief: Quantum phenomena → technologies → defence and government use cases

## Question
Which quantum phenomena are being actively researched for industrial and
commercial use, which technologies do they underpin (or credibly could), and
which defence and government use cases do those technologies support, with
an honest read on how close each link is to reality?

### Sub-questions
1. **Phenomena map.** Which quantum phenomena and behaviours underpin
   established ("first-wave") technologies, and which are showing recent
   promise and active research for industrial or commercial use? Produce a
   broad, reviewable list the user can shortlist.
2. **Phenomenon → technology.** For each phenomenon, which technologies does
   recent research (papers, reviews, roadmaps) connect it to, and what
   applications do the authors themselves claim?
3. **Reality check.** For each link, how mature is it (lab demo, prototype,
   field trial, product), and where does the claimed application look like
   speculation or interest-building rather than an evidenced path?
4. **Technology → use case.** Which defence and government use cases do
   these technologies map to, with priority on the three areas the user's
   prior scan identified: post-quantum cryptography (PQC), quantum sensing,
   and quantum-inspired methods?
5. **Where the three priority areas sit in the chain.** How do PQC and
   quantum-inspired methods (which run on classical hardware) relate to the
   underlying phenomena, so the whitepaper's framing is technically accurate?

## Purpose & audience
Evidence base for a Deloitte whitepaper on quantum in defence and government,
written by the user. The output should help the user review and shortlist
phenomena, then tell a "phenomenon → technology → use case" story that is
technically accurate and separates evidenced paths from speculative ones.
Readers: defence and public-sector clients and Deloitte practitioners;
mixed technical depth.

## Scope
- **Time window:** none set for phenomena foundations; for "recent promise"
  and maturity evidence, weight to 2023 – Oct 2026 and flag older items.
- **Region:** UK / Europe first for programmes and use cases, then US, NATO
  and allies. Physics research is global.
- **In scope:** quantum phenomena with technology relevance (computing,
  sensing, timing, communications, materials, energy); research papers and
  reviews that state applications; defence and government use cases;
  maturity evidence (trials, products, programmes).
- **Out of scope:** market sizing, vendor selection, detailed contract
  tracking (covered by the prior scan in `inbox/`), mathematical detail of
  PQC algorithms.

## Assumptions
- "Quantum phenomena" is read broadly: textbook effects (superposition,
  entanglement, tunnelling, spin, quantised energy levels) plus emergent and
  many-body effects (superconductivity, topological states, squeezing,
  Rydberg blockade, etc.) that recent research ties to applications.
- The phenomenon map is meant for the user to shortlist, so it is wide
  rather than deep; deeper tracing goes to the three priority areas.
- PQC is classical cryptography. It belongs in the chain as a *response* to
  a quantum technology (cryptanalytically relevant quantum computers), not
  as an application of a phenomenon. The report frames it that way.
- Use cases are illustrative of what the evidence supports, not a
  validated demand view; the prior scan covers client demand.
- Deliverables are a brief plus a report; both feed the user's own
  whitepaper drafting rather than being the whitepaper.
